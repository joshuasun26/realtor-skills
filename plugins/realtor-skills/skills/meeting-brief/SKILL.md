# Meeting Brief — read it in the car

The whole value is that it is short enough to read in the five minutes before walking in.
One page. If it runs to three, it will not be read and it produced nothing.

Reads `data/contacts.csv`, `data/pipeline.csv`, `records/sent-log.md`,
`data/market/`, and `listings/` if a property is involved.

---

## Ask first

What kind of meeting, with whom, when, and where. A listing appointment, a buyer
consultation, a showing, and a coffee with another agent need different briefs, and
producing the wrong one wastes the run.

## The brief

**1. Who — three lines.** Name, how the agent knows them, and the single most important
piece of context. Not a biography.

**2. History — the last three touches.** What was sent, when, and what they replied. Pull
from `records/sent-log.md`. This is the part agents most want and most often walk in
without.

**3. What they said they want**, in their own words if the record has them. Their words
beat any summary.

**4. The property, if there is one.** Address, verified specs from `listing-intake` or the
MLS, days on market, price history, and anything in the compliance notes the agent needs
to know before standing in the room.

**5. The numbers — MLS-sourced, dated.** For a listing appointment: recent comparable
sales with addresses, close dates, and prices; current actives; median days to sell. Every
figure from `market-pull`, every one carrying its as-of date.

**Never bring an aggregator estimate into a listing appointment.** The seller has already
seen the aggregator's number, and the agent's entire credibility in that room rests on
being the person with the real data. Bringing the same estimate back is the worst possible
outcome. If MLS data cannot be pulled this session, say so plainly and say the appointment
needs it — do not fill the section with something softer.

**6. Three questions to ask.** Real ones, specific to this person and this property. This
section makes the meeting go well more than any of the data does.

**7. One thing to watch.** The likely objection, the thing they were unhappy about last
time, the competing agent, the timeline pressure. One, not five.

## Rules

- **Everything is sourced.** Every number cites where it came from and when. Run
  `source-check`. An agent quoting a wrong number in a listing appointment loses the
  listing on the spot.
- **No valuation opinion in the brief.** Provide the comparables and let the agent form
  the opinion. That is their license and their judgment.
- **Nothing about the client's demographics, family composition, national origin,
  religion, or anything protected.** Not in a private internal brief either — it has no
  legitimate use, and a discoverable file containing it is a liability.
- **Do not invent history.** If there is no record of prior contact, say "no prior contact
  recorded." That is useful information; a fabricated warm history is a disaster in the
  room.
- If the meeting is with another agent about a co-listing or a referral, include their
  brokerage and anything the record holds about prior dealings, and nothing speculative.

## Format

One page. Markdown, and a PDF if they want it on a phone. Bold the three or four things
that matter most so it survives a 90-second skim.

## Chains from / into

Reads `lead-intake`, `market-pull`, `listing-intake`, `contact-import` output. Uses
`source-check`. Feeds `sphere-message` for the follow-up afterward.
