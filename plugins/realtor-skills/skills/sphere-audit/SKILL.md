# Sphere Audit — find the deals already in the database

Most agents' next transaction is already in their phone. This skill finds it, and finds
the reason it has not happened yet.

Reads `data/contacts.csv`. If it does not exist, run `contact-import` first.

---

## Segment

Sort every contact into one tier. Do not let anything land in more than one.

| Tier | Who | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Past clients, plus anyone who has referred business | 8–12 touches a year |
| **B** | Real relationships with no transaction yet — friends, neighbors, repeat conversations | 4–6 a year |
| **C** | Consented leads and open house visitors, no relationship yet | Sequence-driven |
| **D** | No consent, no relationship, or origin unclear | Nothing automated. Ever. |

Tier D exists so it can be excluded, not worked. It never enters a bulk send from any
skill in this library.

## Score each contact

Score on what is actually predictive, and show your reasoning per contact so the agent can
disagree:

- **Recency** — days since `last_touch`. Past 12 months on an A-tier contact is a problem
  and should be named as one.
- **Depth** — has there been a transaction, a referral, or a real conversation
- **Reachability** — do we have a phone, an email, a birthday, a close date
- **Life-stage signal** — anything recorded in notes that suggests a move: a growing
  family, a job change, a rental they own, an aging parent, a home they have outgrown.
  Only what is actually written down. **Never infer a life stage from a name, an address,
  or a demographic guess** — that is both wrong and a fair housing problem.
- **Anniversary proximity** — past clients at 5, 7, and 10 years from close are the
  highest-probability sellers in most databases

## Report

Lead with three lists, short enough to act on today:

**1. The ten most valuable neglected relationships.** A-tier, no touch in 6+ months, with
the last known context on each. This list is the whole point of the skill. If the agent
does nothing else, they call these ten.

**2. The data gaps that cost the most.** Usually birthdays, close dates, and missing
mobile numbers, with a count on each and one practical way to fill them in.

**3. The anniversary and milestone queue for the next 90 days.** Names and dates.

Then the summary numbers: total contacts, tier counts, how many have no touch recorded in
a year, how many are excluded from sends for consent reasons.

## What NOT to do

- **Do not rank contacts by estimated wealth, home value, neighborhood, or anything
  derived from where they live.** It is a fair housing exposure and it is a bad predictor.
  Rank on relationship depth and recency, which are both better and safe.
- Do not propose a mass reactivation blast to everyone who has gone cold. Cold contacts
  come back through individual messages, and a blast to 400 people who have not heard from
  the agent in three years produces unsubscribes and spam complaints, not appointments.
- Do not delete anything. Flag it, tag it, and let the agent decide.
- Do not write a number about the agent's business into the report that you did not
  compute from the file in front of you.

## Cadence

Run quarterly, not weekly. The database does not change fast enough to justify more, and a
monthly audit becomes noise the agent stops reading.
