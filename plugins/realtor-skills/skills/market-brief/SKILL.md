# Market Brief — the numbers turned into something a client can use

The brief is the highest-leverage recurring piece an agent produces. It is proof they know
their market, it justifies the monthly email, and it gives every follow-up a reason to
exist that is not "just checking in."

Reads `data/market/<area-slug>-<YYYY-MM>.md`, `profile/AGENT.md`, `profile/VOICE.md`.

**If the data file does not exist for the current period, stop and run `market-pull`
first.** Do not write a brief from a previous month's numbers and do not write around the
absence of data.

---

## Structure

**1. The headline — one sentence.** What actually changed. Not "the market remains
dynamic." Something like: "Median sold price held flat while homes took 11 days longer to
sell than a year ago." If nothing changed, say nothing changed. That is a real finding and
saying it builds more trust than manufacturing a trend.

**2. The numbers table.** Four to six metrics, each with the current value, the
year-over-year comparison, and the sample size. Not every metric from the pull — pick the
ones that moved and the ones a normal person understands.

**3. What it means if you are buying.** Two or three sentences, concrete. More choices,
less competition, room to negotiate — or the opposite. Never a prediction.

**4. What it means if you are selling.** Same treatment. Pricing, timing, what condition
is worth.

**5. The honest caveat.** Where the data is thin, where the area definition matters,
what would change the picture. Including this makes the confident parts more believable.

**6. Sources block.** MLS name, area definition verbatim, date range, pull date. Every
time, at the foot of the brief.

## Rules that keep it defensible

- **Never predict.** No "prices will rise", no "now is the time to buy", no "we expect".
  Describe what happened. Interpretation of the past is a service; forecasting is a
  liability.
- **Never say "it's a good time to buy" or "a good time to sell."** It is a good time for
  some people and a bad time for others, and saying so is more useful and more honest.
- **Median, not average**, and say which. One expensive sale moves an average and tells a
  false story.
- **Sample size travels with every percentage.** "Down 12%" on nine sales is noise.
  Report the nine.
- **Year-over-year, not month-over-month**, unless there is a specific reason. Real
  estate is seasonal and MoM mostly measures the calendar.
- **Do not blend property types** without saying so.
- Every number routes through `source-check`. Every time.
- Every neighborhood or area description routes through `compliance-check` — this is the
  piece where "desirable area" and school-quality claims sneak in most often.

## Length and format

- **Email version:** 250 to 400 words. Nobody reads more. Table plus four short sections.
- **One-page PDF version:** same content, the agent's brand, the numbers set larger, the
  sources block at the foot.
- **Three-line text version:** the headline, one number, and one question. This is what
  actually gets replies.

## Voice

Run `agent-voice`. The market brief is where generated copy is most obvious, because
every agent in the country sends one and most of them read identically. The agent's actual
register is the entire differentiator. Scan the draft for banned words before delivering.

## Chains from / into

`sphere-message`, `social-caption`, `meeting-brief`, `open-house-followup`.
