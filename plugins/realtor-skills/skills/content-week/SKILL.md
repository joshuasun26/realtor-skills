# Content Week — plan from the real business, not from a topic list

The failure mode this skill exists to prevent: producing content about real estate in
general, when the agent's actual listings, actual open house, and actual market data are
sitting right there unused.

**Start with what exists.** Only invent a topic when the real inventory is genuinely
empty.

---

## Step 1 — Take inventory before planning anything

Read, in this order:

- `listings/` — active listings, upcoming open houses, anything recently sold or pending
- `data/market/` — is the current month's data pulled? Has anything moved?
- `records/sent-log.md` and prior weeks' plans — what already went out, so nothing repeats
- The agent's calendar for the week, if they will share it

Then ask them two questions and nothing more: **what is happening this week, and what
have people been asking you lately.** The second question produces the best content in
this whole library, because it is a real question from a real person.

## Step 2 — The week

Three to five posts. **Not seven.** An agent who commits to five and delivers five beats
one who commits to seven and stops in week two. Say so if they push for more.

A workable default shape:

| Slot | Type | Built by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A property — listing, open house, or just sold | `listing-carousel` |
| 2 | The market numbers | `market-carousel` |
| 3 | An answer to a real question they were asked | written here |
| 4 | Something human — the agent, the work, the week | written here |
| 5 | The buydown or process explainer, if a listing supports it | `listing-carousel` B |

**Spacing rules:** never two property posts back to back, never the listing carousel and
its buydown carousel on the same day, and put the human post mid-week where the feed is
least crowded.

## Step 3 — Produce

For each slot, call the skill that owns it. Do not rebuild carousel logic here.

- Property posts → `listing-carousel` → `carousel-render`
- Market posts → `market-carousel` → `carousel-render`
- Every post → `social-caption`
- Everything → `compliance-check` and `source-check` before delivery

## Step 4 — Deliver a calendar, not a pile

| Day | Time | Post | Assets | Caption | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus, for each post that involves another agent's listing: the credit and tag block, and a
note to invite them as a collaborator. **That block is the distribution**, not a courtesy.

## Honest guidance to give the agent, once

- **Their own follower graph is probably not who they want.** Most agents' followings were
  built years ago around something else and do not engage with real estate. The reliable
  route to the right audience is tagging and crediting other agents and brokerages on
  property posts, which borrows audiences that are dense with exactly the right people.
- **Consistency beats production value.** Three modest posts a week for a year beats
  twelve polished ones in January.
- **Video outperforms static almost everywhere** — but a static carousel that ships beats
  a video that does not. Build what they will actually do.
- If they have posted nothing for two months, do not plan twenty posts. Plan three, for
  one week, and see if they run.

## Track it

Append the week's plan to `content/plan-<YYYY-Www>.md`. Once a month, pull the actual
results the agent can see — reach, saves, shares — and report which formats worked. Only
numbers they can point at in their own analytics; do not estimate performance.

## Self-improvement loop

Before ending a run of this skill, review the run:

1. Did any step fail, stall, or need a workaround you had to invent?
2. Did the user correct, reject, or rewrite something meaningful in the output?
3. Did you discover something a future run would want to know (a path that moved, a
   tool that replaced another, a preference they stated out loud)?

If yes to any, propose a specific edit to this SKILL.md in one or two lines and ask
whether to apply it. Propose only changes that would alter a future run's behavior --
skip cosmetic rewording, and never propose more than two edits at once.

Do not edit this file without their go-ahead. If they say no, drop it and do not re-raise
the same suggestion in a later run of the same session.
